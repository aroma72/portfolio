Content Queen researches a topic, writes the script, reviews its own work, illustrates, narrates, renders and QA-checks the result, then publishes it to a learning platform with a quiz built in. About 50 minutes and under a dollar per video, with no one in the loop.

Case study · Content Queen · 2026

The problem

A good explainer video takes a team days: research, scripting, illustration, voiceover, editing, review. For a fast-moving AI curriculum that was too slow and too expensive, and quality varied from video to video.

I set out to build a pipeline that could make a video to a consistent standard from a single request, and that would refuse to call a bad video finished.

How it works

Seven stages, each with a gate

A request comes in as a Slack mention and becomes a Notion ticket. From there, nothing moves to the next stage until the current one has proven its output, and no money is spent before a check passes.

  1. 01ResearchGrounds the topic in sources and picks the teaching approach before any writing starts.
  2. 02ScriptOne spoken sentence per scene, following a single character named Ali through one story.
  3. 03ReviewA separate AI reviewer grades the script against a written standard and sends back fixes.
  4. 04ProduceIllustrations, narration and a frame-by-frame render. The cost is quoted up front, and spending is refused without a budget.
  5. 05QASeven automated checks plus a 7-factor rubric. Anything that scores under 4.9 / 7 gets remade.
  6. 06StoreUploaded to Drive with a checksum verified before any local file is deleted.
  7. 07PublishServed through a learning-platform API, with the quiz timed to the exact second.

Hard problems

The worst bugs said "done"

In an AI pipeline, the dangerous failures don't crash. They report success without doing the work. These were the hardest to find.

Problem

A third of a video was blank

Four of twelve scenes rendered as empty frames. The duration and audio were both right, and no error was raised.

Fix

Check the output, not the exit code

A frame sensor samples every scene and fails the build on a blank one. Completeness is checked per item, never in aggregate.

Problem

A reviewer that never approved

The AI script reviewer approved 0 of 43 drafts, so the redraft loop could never end.

Fix

Make the standard reachable

Every note is now tagged blocker or minor, and "ready" means no blockers. The next run passed after one redraft.

Problem

Redrafts broke what worked

Asking the model to "leave the other scenes alone" didn't work. Each round fixed five scenes and broke up to seven others.

Fix

Patches merged in code

Redrafts now return only the edits, and code applies them. A scene no one mentioned can't change.

Problem

The bot answered itself

The Slack bot read its own reply as a new request, which could have meant an unbounded loop of paid renders.

Fix

Three independent guards

The bot detects its own messages, every ticket gets a dedupe key, and there's a spend ceiling where silence never counts as approval.

What I took from it
Encode correctness in structure. Never just request it.
"Please don't leave scenes blank."A validator that rejects the shape.
"Please don't overspend."A gate that runs before the money.
"Please don't touch the other scenes."A patch that's merged in code.
