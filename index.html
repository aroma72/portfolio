The problem: good lessons were slow and expensive to make

A good explainer video takes a team days: research, scripting, illustration, voiceover, editing, review. For a fast-moving AI curriculum that was too slow and too expensive, and quality varied from video to video.

I set out to build a pipeline that makes videos to a consistent standard from a single request, and that refuses to call a bad video finished.

Hard problems

The worst bugs said "done"

In an AI pipeline, the dangerous failures don't crash. They report success without doing the work. These were the hardest to find.

Silent failure

A third of a video was blank

Four of twelve scenes rendered as empty frames. The duration and audio were both right, and no error was raised.

Fix: a frame sensor samples every scene and fails the build on a blank one. Completeness is checked per item, never in aggregate.

Stuck loop

A reviewer that never approved

0 / 43

drafts approved, so the redraft loop could never end.

Fix: notes are tagged blocker or minor, and "ready" means no blockers. The next run passed after one redraft.

Churn

Redrafts broke what worked

"Leave the other scenes alone" didn't work. Each round fixed five scenes and broke up to seven others.

Fix: redrafts return only the edits, and code applies them. A scene no one mentioned can't change.

Money bug

The bot answered itself

The Slack bot read its own reply as a new request, which could have meant an unbounded loop of paid renders.

Fix: self-detection, a dedupe key on every ticket, and a spend ceiling where silence never counts as approval.

What I took from it
Encode correctness in structure. Never just request it.
"Please don't leave scenes blank."A validator that rejects the shape.
"Please don't overspend."A gate that runs before the money.
"Please don't touch the other scenes."A patch that's merged in code.
